# Chapter 5

She had not wanted anything intimate to happen while Barry was on his date with a sighted woman. The thought of it sat in her stomach like a stone, sharp and unwelcome, and when he called to arrange their second swimming lesson she found herself saying no before she’d really thought it through.

Barry sounded surprised on the phone. She could hear the shift in his voice, the way he tried to play it light. “What, seriously? You’re bailing on me?”

“I can’t make it,” she said. “Something’s come up.”

He didn’t push hard, but there was something in his tone that made her feel like he already knew — or at least suspected — that the “something” had everything to do with the sighted woman he’d mentioned. She didn’t want to admit that. She didn’t want to admit any of it.

Later, in the quiet of her flat, the anger and frustration settled into a cold, tight thing. She wanted Barry to understand, but she didn’t know how to say it without sounding like she was asking for more than she was allowed to have. She was angry with him for mentioning the date at all. She was angry with herself for caring.

When they next met, she told him plainly. She let him know how it had made her feel — not gently, not softened, but honestly, the words spilling out before she could rehearse them. It was a risk. It meant showing him the part of herself she usually kept locked away.

Barry listened. He didn’t laugh. He didn’t brush it off. For that she was grateful, even if gratitude wasn’t what she’d expected to feel.

Then he made a joke about condoms.

She stared at him, the words hanging in the air between them. It wasn’t funny. Not now. Not after everything she’d just said. The humor he thought might lighten the moment only dug the knife in deeper. She didn’t find it clever or charming or kind. She found it dismissive, and she let him know that too — her voice tight, her jaw set.

Afterward, she walked away without looking back. The cancellation of the swimming lesson still stood. She didn’t call to reverse it. Some things needed to stay cancelled, she told herself, at least until the knot inside her loosened enough to breathe again.
